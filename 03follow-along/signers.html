Who Were the Signers

The People Who Signed the Declaration of Independence

Thomas Jefferson
Thomas Jefferson (1743–1826) was an American statesman, diplomat, architect, and the third President of the United States from 1801 to 1809. He was the primary author of the Declaration of Independence in 1776. Before his presidency, he served as the Governor of Virginia, the U.S. Minister to France, the first Secretary of State under George Washington, and the second Vice President under John Adams. Jefferson and James Madison founded the Democratic-Republican Party to oppose the Federalists and protect the rights of individual states. During his presidency, he completed the Louisiana Purchase in 1803, which doubled the land area of the United States, and authorized the Lewis and Clark Expedition to map the western territory. After he left office, he founded the University of Virginia.
John Adams
John Adams (1735–1826) was an American statesman, lawyer, and the second President of the United States from 1797 to 1801. As a delegate from Massachusetts to the Continental Congress, he led the movement for independence from Great Britain and helped draft the Declaration of Independence. During the Revolutionary War, he worked as a diplomat in Europe and secured loans and treaties from the Netherlands and Great Britain. He served two terms as the first Vice President under George Washington. As a member of the Federalist Party, Adams supported a strong central government and an independent judiciary. During his presidency, he built up the United States Navy, avoided a full war with France through diplomacy, and signed the controversial Alien and Sedition Acts.
John Hancock
President of the Second Continental Congress and the first person to sign the Declaration. His large signature at the top center made his name a common term for any signature.
Benjamin Franklin
A 70-year-old scientist, writer, printer, and diplomat. He served on the Committee of Five that drafted the Declaration and later secured the French alliance during the Revolutionary War.
Samuel Adams
Second cousin to John Adams and founder of the Sons of Liberty. He organized resistance to British taxation in Boston before signing the Declaration.
Roger Sherman
A member of the drafting committee and the only person to sign all four major founding documents of the United States: the Continental Association, the Declaration of Independence, the Articles of Confederation, and the U.S. Constitution.
Benjamin Rush
A physician and social reformer who served as Surgeon General of the Continental Army. He later helped reconcile John Adams and Thomas Jefferson.
Richard Henry Lee
The delegate who introduced the Lee Resolution on June 7, 1776, which declared the colonies independent from Great Britain and led directly to the writing of the Declaration.
Charles Carroll of Carrollton
The only Catholic signer and the wealthiest delegate in Congress. He outlived all other signers and died in 1832 at age 95.
Robert Morris
A Philadelphia merchant known as the "Financier of the Revolution." He used his personal credit and shipping networks to fund the Continental Army and signed the Declaration, the Articles of Confederation, and the U.S. Constitution.
Famous Non-Signers
George Washington and Alexander Hamilton did not sign because they were in New York with the Continental Army. James Madison was serving in the Virginia state legislature. Robert Livingston helped draft the Declaration on the Committee of Five, but New York recalled him before the official signing took place.
