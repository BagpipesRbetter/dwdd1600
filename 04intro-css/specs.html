Armored Vehicle Classifications and Specifications

Evaluating World War II tanks requires examining the balance of the armored triangle: firepower, protection, and mobility. Every nation made distinct engineering compromises depending on factory capacity, rail transport limits, and combat terrain.

Primary Tank Classifications

Armored vehicles during the conflict were organized into distinct operational roles within armored and infantry divisions:

  • Light Tanks: Built for reconnaissance, screening, and flanking maneuvers (such as the American M3 Stuart and M22 Locust).
  • Medium Tanks: All-purpose combat vehicles balancing mobility, armor, and gun power (such as the M4A2 Sherman, T-34/85, and Panzer IV Ausf. H).
  • Heavy Tanks: Designed to penetrate fortified lines and engage enemy armor at long range (such as the German Tiger I and Soviet KV-2).
  • Tank Destroyers and Assault Guns: Casemate or open-topped vehicles carrying heavy guns on existing chassis (such as the StuG III Ausf. G and M18 Hellcat).

Most Produced Armored Vehicles of World War II

Industrial output determined the availability of armor on the battlefield. The following list ranks the five most produced tracked armored fighting vehicles of the war:

  1. Soviet T-34 Medium Tank (approximately 84,000 built across all variants)
  2. American M4 Sherman Medium Tank (49,234 built)
  3. Soviet T-70 Light Tank (8,226 built)
  4. German StuG III Assault Gun (over 10,000 built)
  5. German Panzerkampfwagen IV Medium Tank (8,553 built)

Armored Warfare Technical Glossary

Understanding technical specification tables requires familiarity with standard ordnance and armor terminology used by ordnance engineers and historians:

Sloped Armor
Armor plates mounted at an inclined angle rather than vertically, increasing the effective line-of-sight thickness against incoming projectiles and encouraging ricochets.
Rolled Homogeneous Armor (RHA)
Standardized hot-rolled steel plate used both to construct tanks and as the baseline unit of measurement (in millimeters) for anti-tank gun penetration performance.
Armor-Piercing Composite Rigid (APCR)
High-velocity anti-tank ammunition featuring a dense tungsten carbide core surrounded by a lightweight aluminum alloy body to maximize kinetic penetration at short to medium ranges.
Torsion Bar Suspension
A suspension system using transverse steel bars anchored to the hull floor that twist under load, providing smooth cross-country travel for road wheels.
